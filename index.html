NeuroTrack: brain health, measured against your own baseline

A short cognitive and functional check, adapted to the person's language and culture, repeated over time. Each person is compared with their own two-session baseline and with people like them in age, education, language, digital familiarity, hearing and vision. Change is confirmed with a repeat test before anyone is referred, and results link to the risk factors the person has agreed to share.

New here? Take the five-minute tour, or go straight to the programme dashboard and press Play.

Held-out synthetic test cohort, n = 1,000, 3-year follow-up. Comparison is with a once-a-year screen using demographically adjusted norms.

How it works

1 · Assess

10-minute tablet or assisted check in Arabic or English: word recall, symbol–digit, sequencing, category fluency, daily function, mood and sleep.

2 · Baseline

Two sessions about 2 weeks apart form a personal baseline and measure reliability.

3 · Detect change

Regression-based change scores adjust for practice, age, education, language and time.

4 · Confirm and triage

A flag triggers a repeat test. Only a change that persists is referred, and clinicians review every referral first.

5 · Act

Plain-language Arabic or English report: top modifiable factors, treatable causes to check and the right service.

Accuracy and referral burden

Timeliness

Detection lag is the time from when true decline reached 0.5 SD to referral, after any retest. Time to clinical assessment adds a modelled wait of 28 days for primary care and 14 days for a specialist.

Fairness across language, age and education

The unadjusted cut-off refers too many people with little formal schooling and falsely reassures highly educated people. NeuroTrack keeps error rates similar across groups.

Safety, oversight and data governance

Risk identification, not diagnosis

Every output is labelled as a risk signal. Participants never see a diagnostic label such as MCI or dementia.

Human in the loop

Primary-care and specialist referrals, and mood-related flags, wait in a clinician queue. They are released only after a decision is recorded: approve, downgrade, escalate or ask for more information. Every decision is logged.

Uncertainty disclosed

Reports say how often a change of that size happens in people whose brain health is stable, and why the change is confirmed before anyone acts.

Confounders handled

Low mood (PHQ-9 of 10 or more) or very short sleep leads to a wellbeing review and a later repeat, not a neurology referral.

Consent by category

Participants choose which data inform their risk context: clinical, wearable, lifestyle, hearing and vision, family and genomic. Genomic results never trigger a referral on their own and come with an offer of genetic counselling.

Transparent rules

Every threshold sits in one configuration file that a clinical governance group can review. Each triage decision returns the reasons that produced it.

Data protection

Designed around UAE Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on ICT in Health Fields, which requires confidentiality and in-country storage of health data, and around the Abu Dhabi Healthcare Information and Cyber Security Standard, the DoH Policy on AI in Healthcare and the Malaffi exchange. Data would be hosted in-country, encrypted, pseudonymised for analytics, with role-based access and audit logs. Compliance is to be confirmed with DoH during the pilot.

Fairness monitoring

Sensitivity, specificity and false referrals are reported by language, age and education at every model update. Any subgroup drifting outside agreed bounds triggers recalibration.

How to use this app

Each walkthrough takes under five minutes. The report-language switch in the left menu changes participant reports between English and Arabic on every screen. All data is synthetic.

Five-minute tour

  1. Read the headline results on Overview.
  2. Open the Programme dashboard and press Play. Referrals build up and the two medians in the change chart separate over three years.
  3. Set Education to 0–6 yrs, then 13+ yrs. Referral rates stay comparable between groups.
  4. In the Threshold explorer, drag the cut-off. A single low score trades missed decliners for false alarms; NeuroTrack's star sits above that curve.
  5. On Participant journey, choose “Low-mood dip, resolved on retest” and see a flag clear with no referral. Switch the report to Arabic.
  6. On Live session scoring, press Simulate decline, then Simulate a low-mood day, and compare the decisions.

Reading the numbers

Change zToday's score against what was expected from the person's own baseline. 0 means as expected; −1.5 or lower is a possible change.
About N in 100How many people with stable brain health would score this low by chance. A smaller number is a stronger signal.
Norm zThe person against others of the same age, education, language and digital familiarity, regardless of history.
RetestA repeat check 2–6 weeks after a first flag. Only a change that persists is referred.
PAFPopulation attributable fraction from the Lancet Commission 2024: the share of dementia linked to a factor across a population. Used to rank actions, not to predict one person's risk.

Programme manager

  1. Filter the dashboard to your population.
  2. Drag the timeline to the current month and read the headline numbers.
  3. Check sessions needing action for next cycle's workload.
  4. Scan the heatmap for a group with unusual referral rates.

Nurse or health worker

  1. Open Live session scoring and select the participant.
  2. Set each slider to the scores achieved, with mood and sleep.
  3. Follow the Next step box.
  4. Never present a result as a diagnosis.

Clinician

  1. Open the Clinician review queue; specialist cases come first.
  2. Select a case to see its trajectory and reasons.
  3. Approve, downgrade, escalate or ask for more information, with a note.
  4. Check the participant's report on Participant journey.
